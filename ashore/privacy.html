Ashore

Support

Ashore helps you make the most of a few hours in port and get back to the ship on time. Tell it your all-aboard time and it shows a live countdown, ideas for places that fit the time you have, and a way back that always keeps a margin in reserve.

Everything stays on your iPhone. Ashore has no account and no server of its own. Your trip, your saved places and your preferences never leave your phone, and the whole app works with no signal.

Where do I find my all-aboard time?
In your cruise line's daily program or app. Ashore asks for it when you add a port. Enter it as the ship's clock shows it, which can differ from the time on shore. Compare it with a clock on board before you leave, and confirm it in the app.

How are the walking times worked out?
From the distance between the ship and each place, with extra margin added. They are estimates. Mark your ship's exact berth in the app for better numbers.

Why is a place missing from my ideas?
Places that would make you late are left out, and listed at the bottom of the results with the reason, so nothing is hidden from you.

Are opening hours and prices included?
Most places do not have recorded opening hours or prices, and the app says so instead of guessing. Check with the venue before you go.

Does it need an internet connection?
No. The guides for more than two hundred ports are stored inside the app. Exchange rates can refresh when you are online, and the date of the rate is always shown beside every figure.

How do I see or delete what Ashore has stored?
Open You, then Your data. You can export everything as a file or delete it all in one tap.

What is Meet travelers?
An optional tool in the Toolkit tab, for 18 and over. It is off until you turn it on. You choose what you are looking for (new friends, board games, a meal, or meeting someone), add a nickname and, if you like, up to five pictures of yourself and whether you are male or female. While it is on, Ashore uses Bluetooth, even when the app is closed, to notice other travelers close by who are looking for the same thing, and tells you quietly. If you each say yes, your WhatsApp or phone numbers are swapped the next time your phones are close. The number you share can be picked from your contacts with the system picker (Ashore receives only the one number you tap and never asks for access to your contacts), pasted as a WhatsApp link, or typed. You can hide, block or report anyone, and turn it off at any moment.

What is "What to bring"?
For a port that is within about ten days, Ashore checks the forecast for the hours you will be ashore and suggests things like sunscreen, a rain jacket or water. If there is no forecast (no signal, or the port is further away) it shows nothing rather than guessing.

How do I bring in my itinerary?
Tap Import an itinerary. Choose a screenshot or photo of it (from your cruise line's app or website), a PDF, or text you copied. Ashore reads it on your phone, never sends it anywhere, shows you what it found, and lets you correct it before anything is added.

Can I rely on it to get me back?
Ashore helps you plan, but you are responsible for being back on board in time. Always confirm the all-aboard time with your ship.

Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 2026

Ashore does not collect, transmit, or share any data about you. There is no account system, no analytics, and no advertising.

What stays on your phone: Your itinerary, saved places, places you visited, ratings, notes, preferences, settings, and anything the app has learned from your choices. You can see all of it, export it as a file, or delete it from You, then Your data. Deleting removes it from your phone and cancels reminders.

Location: If you allow it, Ashore reads your location once, only when you ask, for example to find a guide near you, to mark where the ship is, or to work out how far places are. It is not stored, not shared, and never tracked. The one exception is a position you choose to mark as the ship's berth: that is saved on your phone, as part of that stop, until you clear it. You can use the whole app without allowing location.

Notifications: Ashore asks to send reminders before all aboard when you add a port. They are scheduled on your phone from the time you entered. Nothing is sent from a server.

Reading your itinerary: When you bring in a screenshot, photo or PDF, the text is read on your phone using the text recognition built into iOS. The picture is not stored by Ashore and is not sent anywhere.

Meet travelers (optional, 18 and over): This is off until you turn it on, and it stops at the time you choose (one hour, twelve hours, or up to two weeks) or when you turn it off. While it is on, Ashore uses Bluetooth, including in the background, so that phones close to you can find each other directly, with no server and no internet connection. People near you who are looking for at least one of the same things can see the nickname, short line, interests, optional gender (male or female) and picture count you chose. They can ask your phone for your pictures (up to five, which you choose with the system photo picker) and they are shown blurred until they choose to look. Your phone number or messaging contact is never part of that card. If you both say yes, each phone sends its contact, encrypted for that one person, the next time the two phones are close, even if Ashore is closed. Who you want to meet is applied on your phone and is not sent to anyone. Ashore keeps a short record on your phone of the people it noticed, so that a yes can be matched later: entries are removed after a day, or after a week for a yes you gave, and the people you block are remembered so they stay blocked. If you save a connection it stays on your phone until you delete it. Delete all data (You, then Your data) removes your profile, pictures, that record and your connections and gives you a new anonymous identity. You can hide, block or report anyone. Ashore cannot verify anyone, people can say things that are not true, and phones within Bluetooth range can read your card while you are visible. Meet in public places and keep an eye on your all-aboard time. One of the things you can say you are looking for is the pineapple, the sign couples use to find other couples; like every other choice it is shown only to people who chose it as well, and it travels with your card the same way.

Let someone know you're okay (optional): A tool in the Toolkit tab, off until you turn it on. You enter a name for yourself and, on each port day, tap once to say you are ashore and okay, and once more when you are back aboard. That record (your name, the stop, the time of your last word, when your next word is due, and a note if you wrote one) is kept on your phone. If you invite a watcher, the record is also written to your own iCloud account and shared with the people who open your invitation link; it goes through Apple's iCloud, not through any server of ours, and we cannot read it. A watcher's phone keeps a copy of your plan and can sound a local alarm if it has heard nothing by all aboard plus the margin you chose; it asks for notification permission only when the watcher turns that alarm on. You can stop sharing at any time, which removes the record from iCloud, and Delete all data removes it too. Without iCloud, or in addition to it, the tool writes a short note you can send with any messaging app.

Split the costs (optional): A tool in the Toolkit tab for a tab you share with the people you travel with: the names you type, each expense (what, how much, in which currency, who paid, who shares it) and the sums the app works out from them. All of it is kept on your phone. If you share a tab, it is written to your own iCloud account and the people who open your invitation link can see it and add expenses to it from their phones; it goes through Apple's iCloud, not through any server of ours, and we cannot read it. The person who made the tab can remove it for everyone; anyone else can leave it. Delete all data removes your tabs, and the ones you made are removed from iCloud. The summary can always be sent as a message to people who are not in the tab.

Who else is in port: Each stop can show how many other cruise ships call there the same day, from the schedules the ports publish. A copy of those schedules is built into the app, and when the phone is online and you allow refreshes, it fetches a newer copy of the same public file; the request carries no account, position or identifier. The ship you name under Your ship stays on your phone and is only used to leave it out of the count and to show you facts about it.

Weather: For a port within about ten days, Ashore asks the weather service built into Apple's operating system for the forecast at the port's arrival point (a place in the destination guide, not your location) to suggest what to bring. The request contains that point and the time window, and nothing that identifies you. Apple's own privacy policy applies to that service.

What uses the internet: Exchange rates: Ashore can refresh a public file of exchange rates when you are online. It sends nothing about you, and you can turn this off in Settings. Maps: map pictures are drawn by Apple Maps. Translation: Apple's on-device translation may download a language the first time you use it. Weather: the forecast described above, only when you view a port within about ten days. Everything else works with no signal.

What data we collect: None at all. The app contains no analytics or advertising SDKs and no crash-reporting service.

Children: Ashore is not directed at children and collects no personal information from anyone. Meet travelers is only for people 18 and older.

Changes: If this policy ever changes, the updated version will be posted here with a new date.
