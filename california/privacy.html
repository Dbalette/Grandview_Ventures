California Water Watch

Support

California Water Watch tracks California's water story with real data on reservoirs, rivers, snowpack, drought, and groundwater, explained in plain English, with sources you can check.

Where does the data come from?
Live public data from official government sources covering reservoirs, rivers, snowpack, drought, and drinking-water quality. The State Water Project allocation is a real, dated official figure, cited rather than live-fetched. Groundwater basin plan status is fetched live from a published state dataset and shown alongside dated, cited context. Each figure in the app is labeled by how solid it is: live but pending agency review, quality reviewed, agency reported, modeled estimate, last known, or no reading observed.

Does this app require an internet connection?
Yes. California Water Watch fetches live readings from public government water-data services to stay current. On iPhone and iPad, when iOS or iPadOS allows it, the app may also refresh its cached readings in the background, using only Apple's own background refresh mechanism. On Mac it refreshes when you open or return to it. On Apple TV it refreshes when you open it and then about every fifteen minutes while the dashboard stays on screen.

Does the app use my location?
No. California Water Watch does not request or use your location. It presents statewide and site-level data for fixed monitoring locations across California.

Does the app send notifications?
Only if you allow them, and only about places you follow. When a place you have saved sets a new record high or low, the app creates a notification on your device. There is no server behind it.

Does the Apple Watch app work on its own?
The Watch app mirrors your saved places and the statewide condition from your iPhone. Your iPhone sends it a fresh snapshot each time the app refreshes while open.

Privacy Policy

Last updated: September 2026

California Water Watch does not collect, transmit, or share any personal data. The app has no account system, no analytics, no advertising, and does not request your location.

What data is stored: Your saved places and preferences, plus the readings the app has downloaded, kept so it can chart trends and show a last-known value when a source is unreachable, are kept locally on your device. None of it is transmitted to us, to Apple, or to any third party.

What the app fetches: The app makes outbound requests to public government water-data services to display current conditions. These are one-way reads of public data. No personal or device information is sent as part of these requests beyond what is inherent to any standard network request. As with any request to any internet server, the operators of those services necessarily receive the network address each request came from, and their handling of it is governed by their own policies. We receive none of it. Two state groundwater datasets, basin boundaries and plan status, are published by the state through a commercial mapping-cloud host, so that host receives the same kind of request when they load, under its own privacy policy. The home screen's map is drawn by Apple Maps, so Apple's map servers receive the same kind of request when the map loads, under Apple's own privacy policy. Your location is not part of those requests.

Notifications: If you allow them, notifications are created entirely on your device when a place you follow sets a new record, by comparing newly fetched public data against what the app saw before. Nothing is pushed from any server, and no notification content leaves your device.

Apple Watch: If you use the Apple Watch app, your iPhone sends it a snapshot of your saved places and the statewide condition with Apple's WatchConnectivity framework. That snapshot stays on your own devices and is never sent to us or to any third party.

Mac and Apple TV: The Mac and Apple TV apps fetch the same public data directly and keep the same local cache on that device. Apple TV also remembers which dashboard boards you pinned.

What data we collect: None. California Water Watch uses no analytics or advertising SDKs and has no account system. Nothing you do in this app is reported to us.

Third parties: The only third parties involved are the public government data services the app reads from and the commercial mapping-cloud host that serves two state groundwater datasets, as described above, and Apple Maps, which draws the home-screen map. We share no data with anyone, because we collect none.

Data retention and deletion: We hold nothing, so there is nothing to request the deletion of. Everything the app stores stays on your device, including your saved places, preferences, downloaded readings, and, on Apple TV, the boards you pinned. Deleting the app from any of your devices, whether iPhone, iPad, Apple Watch, Mac, or Apple TV, removes all of it from that device permanently. The only permission the app asks for is notifications, and you can withdraw it at any time in your device's Settings. Beyond that, because there is no account and nothing is sent to us, there is no data for us to return or erase on your behalf. If you have a question about any of this, write to the address below.

Children's privacy: Because no personal data is collected from any user, this app does not collect personal information from children.
