Colorado River Lower Basin Watch

Support

Colorado River Lower Basin Watch tracks the water story of the Colorado River's Lower Basin, the three states of Arizona, California, and Nevada, with real data on reservoirs, rivers, snowpack, and drought, plus water news, explained in plain English, with sources you can check.

Where does the data come from?
Live public data from official government sources covering reservoir and river levels, mountain snowpack, drought, and active flood alerts across the basin. Each figure in the app is labeled by how solid it is: live, quality reviewed, modeled estimate, last known, or no reading observed.

What is the News tab?
The News tab shows recent Lower Basin water coverage pulled from public news feeds and a commercial news search service, with a sentiment label (positive, neutral, or negative) computed entirely on your device from each headline and snippet. Tapping a headline opens the story in your browser at the address the feed supplied, under that site's own policies. Stories you save stay on your device until you remove them. Unsaved stories are cleared automatically after one year.

Does this app require an internet connection?
Yes. Colorado River Lower Basin Watch fetches live readings from public government water-data services and headlines from public news feeds and a commercial news search service to stay current. On iPhone and iPad, when iOS or iPadOS allows it, the app may also refresh its cached readings in the background, using only Apple's own background refresh mechanism. On Mac it refreshes when you open or return to it. On Apple TV it refreshes when you open it and then about every fifteen minutes while the dashboard stays on screen.

Does the app use my location?
Only for one purpose. If you open the app while an official flood alert is active, it can check whether that alert's warned area covers where you are, so that alert can be shown first. This check happens entirely on your device. Your location is never stored, never transmitted anywhere, and never used for anything else. You can decline the permission prompt and the app works the same, just without that one alert-ordering convenience.

Does the app send notifications?
Only if you allow them. When a place you have saved sets a new record high or low, or when any place crosses a threshold you set for it, the app creates a notification on your device. There is no server behind it.

Does the Apple Watch app work on its own?
Yes. The Watch app shows your saved places and the basin-wide condition. It receives a snapshot from your iPhone whenever the phone refreshes, and it also fetches the same public data itself whenever you open or refresh it or watchOS wakes it in the background, whether or not your iPhone is nearby.

Can I ask my own questions?
On iPhones, iPads, and Macs that support Apple Intelligence, the Explain sheet can answer a question you type, using Apple's on-device model and only the evidence the app shows for that place. The question and the answer never leave your device. On other devices the built-in questions remain.

Privacy Policy

Last updated: September 2026

Colorado River Lower Basin Watch does not collect, transmit, or share any personal data. The app has no account system, no analytics, and no advertising. If you choose to share your location, it is used only on your device, as described below.

What data is stored: Your saved places, saved news stories, alert thresholds, and preferences, plus the readings and headlines the app has downloaded, kept so it can chart trends and show a last-known value when a source is unreachable, are kept locally on your device. None of it is transmitted to us, to Apple, or to any third party.

What the app fetches: The app makes outbound requests to public government water-data services and the news services it reads headlines from to display current conditions and headlines. These are one-way reads of public data. No personal or device information is sent as part of these requests beyond what is inherent to any standard network request. As with any request to any internet server, the operators of those services necessarily receive the network address each request came from, and their handling of it is governed by their own policies. We receive none of it. The requests to the commercial news service carry a key that identifies the app, never you. Several state datasets, such as groundwater basin boundaries and well records, are published by the state through a commercial mapping-cloud host, so that host receives the same kind of request when they load, under its own privacy policy. One canal operator's live reservoir telemetry is likewise served from a commercial cloud application host, which receives the same kind of request under its own privacy policy. The home screen's map is drawn by Apple Maps, so Apple's map servers receive the same kind of request when the map loads, under Apple's own privacy policy. Your location is not part of those requests.

Location: Optional, and used for one purpose only. If an official flood alert is active when you open the app, it can check on your device whether that alert's warned area covers where you are, so that alert can be shown first. Only the yes-or-no result of that check reaches the screen. Your location is never stored, never transmitted anywhere, and never used for anything else. You can decline the permission prompt, or withdraw the permission at any time in your device's Settings, and the app works the same without it, apart from that one alert-ordering convenience.

On-device processing: News sentiment labels are computed entirely on your device with Apple's built-in language framework, and headline text is never sent anywhere for this analysis. On devices that support Apple Intelligence, free-form questions on a place's Explain sheet are answered by Apple's on-device model, and neither the question nor the answer leaves your device.

Notifications: If you allow them, notifications are created entirely on your device when a place you have saved sets a new record, or any place crosses a threshold you set for it, by comparing newly fetched public data against what the app saw before. Nothing is pushed from any server, and no notification content leaves your device.

Apple Watch: If you use the Apple Watch app, your iPhone sends it a snapshot of your saved places and the basin-wide condition with Apple's WatchConnectivity framework, and the Watch can also fetch the same public data directly. That snapshot stays on your own devices and is never sent to us or to any third party.

Mac and Apple TV: The Mac and Apple TV apps fetch the same public data directly and keep the same local cache on that device. Apple TV also remembers which dashboard boards you pinned. The Mac app asks for location for the same single flood-alert purpose, and its Export command writes the app's public readings to a folder you choose, where they are yours to keep or delete.

What data we collect: None. Colorado River Lower Basin Watch uses no analytics or advertising SDKs and has no account system. Nothing you do in this app is reported to us.

Third parties: The only third parties involved are the public government data services the app reads from, the commercial cloud hosts that serve several state and canal-operator datasets, and the news services it reads headlines from, as described above, and Apple Maps, which draws the home-screen map. We share no data with anyone, because we collect none.

Data retention and deletion: We hold nothing, so there is nothing to request the deletion of. Everything the app stores stays on your device, including your saved places, saved news stories, alert thresholds, preferences, downloaded readings and headlines, and, on Apple TV, the boards you pinned. Saved stories stay until you remove them, and unsaved stories are cleared automatically after one year. Your location, when you share it, is used in the moment and never stored. Deleting the app from any of your devices, whether iPhone, iPad, Apple Watch, Mac, or Apple TV, removes all of it from that device permanently. The only permissions the app asks for are location and notifications, and you can withdraw either at any time in your device's Settings. Beyond that, because there is no account and nothing is sent to us, there is no data for us to return or erase on your behalf. If you have a question about any of this, write to the address below.

Children's privacy: Because no personal data is collected from any user, this app does not collect personal information from children.
