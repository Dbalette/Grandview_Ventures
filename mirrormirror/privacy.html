Mirror Mirror: Golden Ratio

Support and Privacy Policy

Support

Mirror Mirror: Golden Ratio is an entertainment app. It looks at your face through the front camera, or at a photograph you choose, finds 478 landmark points on it, and compares the proportions between those points with the golden ratio, the classical canons of proportion, and your own left to right symmetry. It gives you a score out of 100 and, more importantly, shows you every measurement and every formula behind that score.

Why does it ask for the camera?
To see your face. The video is processed on your phone, frame by frame, by a face landmark model that is built into the app. Nothing is recorded and nothing is uploaded. If you would rather not use the camera at all, choose Use a photo instead and pick a picture from your library, or hold the mirror to a painting instead. Photos are analysed the same way, on the device.

What are the masterpiece readings?
The same measurements applied to six famous painted faces that are built into the app: Leonardo's Mona Lisa, Botticelli's Venus, a self-portrait by Dürer, a portrait of Henry VIII after Holbein, a Roman-era Fayum portrait and Klimt's Adele Bloch-Bauer. They are in the public domain, need no camera and no photograph, are free, and do not use up your free readings. A painter chooses how to light, turn and flatter a face, so treat them as company and not as rivals.

Why do my scores change between readings?
Because the measurements are taken from a two-dimensional picture of a three-dimensional face. Turning the head a few degrees, lifting the chin, smiling, a wide-angle lens held too close, or hair on the forehead all move the landmarks. The mirror averages about a second of video and warns you when your pose was off, but the honest answer is that a reading is good to roughly plus or minus two points. Take three readings in good light and keep the middle one.

Is the golden ratio really the measure of beauty?
No, not really, and the app says so. It is an old and lovely hypothesis, popular with artists and some surgeons, but controlled studies find it a weak predictor of who people actually find attractive. Averageness, symmetry, skin and expression matter more. Treat the number as a parlour game with good arithmetic. The “How the math works” page inside the app lists the published studies, and a short explainer inside the app covers what the golden ratio is.

Does it work offline?
Yes. The app carries everything inside it: the face model, the vision library and its typefaces.

Which phones?
iPhone with iOS 16 or later.

How do I cancel, or get my purchase back?
Three readings are free. Mirror Mirror Premium is an auto-renewing subscription bought through the App Store. To cancel, open Settings on your iPhone, tap your name, then Subscriptions. On a new phone, tap Restore purchases on the paywall. Refunds are handled by Apple.

Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 2026

Mirror Mirror has no server of its own. There is no account, no sign-in, and no database of users or faces anywhere.

Camera and photos: video frames and photos are processed in memory on your device by a face landmark model that is built into the app. No frame, photo, landmark, measurement or score is sent anywhere, stored by us, or kept after you leave the reading. If you tap Save the card, a picture of your result is handed to the share sheet on your own device, and goes nowhere unless you choose where.

Masterpieces: the six paintings you can read with the mirror are stored inside the app and are in the public domain. Choosing one does not use the camera or any photograph of you, and nothing about the choice leaves your device.

Free readings: the app remembers how many free readings you have used by keeping a small counter in its own storage on your device. It is not sent anywhere and is not linked to you.

What we collect: nothing. The app contains no analytics, no advertising, no tracking and no third-party SDKs that phone home. We cannot see who uses it or what they look like.

Third parties: the app contains everything it needs and makes no network request of its own while you use it. The only other party is the App Store, which handles your subscription under Apple's own privacy policy. The open-source notices inside the app list what it is built from.

Permissions: the camera permission is requested only when you tap Open the mirror, and you can revoke it at any time in your phone's Settings.

Children: the app is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect anything from anyone.

Changes: if this policy changes in a way that affects what is processed or where, this page will say so before the change takes effect.
