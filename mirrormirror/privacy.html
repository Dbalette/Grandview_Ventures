Support

Mirror Mirror on The Wall is a small web app that looks at your face through the front camera, finds 478 landmark points on it, and compares the proportions between those points with the golden ratio, the classical canons of proportion, and your own left-to-right symmetry. It gives you a score out of 100 and, more importantly, shows you every measurement and every formula behind that score.

Why does it ask for the camera?
To see your face. The video is processed on your phone, frame by frame, by a face landmark model that runs inside your browser. Nothing is recorded and nothing is uploaded. If you would rather not use the camera at all, choose Use a photo instead and pick a picture from your library; it is analysed the same way, on the device.

Why do my scores change between readings?
Because the measurements are taken from a two-dimensional picture of a three-dimensional face. Turning the head a few degrees, lifting the chin, smiling, a wide-angle lens held too close, or hair on the forehead all move the landmarks. The mirror averages about a second of video and warns you when your pose was off, but the honest answer is that a reading is good to roughly plus or minus two points. Take three readings in good light and keep the middle one.

Is the golden ratio really the measure of beauty?
No, not really, and the app says so. It is an old and lovely hypothesis, popular with artists and some surgeons, but controlled studies find it a weak predictor of who people actually find attractive. Averageness, symmetry, skin and expression matter more. Treat the number as a parlour game with good arithmetic. The methodology page gives every source.

Does it work offline?
Yes. The iPhone app carries everything inside it. The web version keeps the page, the face model and the vision library in the browser cache after the first visit.

Which browsers?
Safari on iOS 15 or later, Chrome on Android, and any current desktop browser. The camera needs a secure page (https), which this is.

Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 2026

Mirror Mirror has no server of its own. There is no account, no sign-in, and no database of users or faces anywhere.

Camera and photos: video frames and photos are processed in memory on your device by a face landmark model that runs inside your browser. No frame, photo, landmark, measurement or score is sent anywhere, stored by us, or kept after you close the page. If you tap Save the card, a picture of your result is written to your own device, and only there.

What we collect: nothing. The app contains no analytics, no advertising, no tracking and no third-party SDKs that phone home. We cannot see who uses it or what they look like.

Third-party resources: the iPhone app contains everything it needs, including the face landmark library, the face model and its typefaces, and makes no network request of its own while you use it. The only other traffic is the App Store handling your subscription, which Apple's privacy policy covers. The web version downloads the vision library from the jsDelivr content network and the face model from Google's public model storage the first time you open it; those requests carry the ordinary information any web request does (your IP address and browser type) and are covered by those providers' own policies. No image data is ever part of those requests, and after the first visit the web version works offline. The open-source notices list what the app is built from.

Permissions: the camera permission is requested only when you tap Open the mirror, and you can revoke it at any time in your browser or phone settings.

Children: the app is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect anything from anyone.

Changes: if this policy changes in a way that affects what is processed or where, this page will say so before the change takes effect.
